## Workshop Abstract

The concept of passive brain-computer interfaces (BCIs) was formally introduced in 2011. It describes systems that decode naturally-occurring brain activity without volitional user effort to enrich human-machine interaction with implicit information about cognitive and affective state. Fifteen years on, passive BCIs have become a recognized subfield, with demonstrated applications in workload monitoring, error detection, and neuroadaptive technology. Yet widespread real-world deployment remains elusive. This workshop takes stock: What theoretical and methodological foundations have held up? Where has progress stalled, and why? What does the next decade require in terms of universal classifiers, unobtrusive hardware, ecological validity, and integration with AI systems? Through a combination of retrospective talks, presentations of research insights, and structured interactive discussion, we aim to produce a shared critical assessment of the field's state and a community-driven agenda for what passive BCI research should prioritize next.

## Workshop Goals

1. Provide a critical retrospective of 15 years of passive BCI research: what the field delivered and what remains to be explored.
2. Present current work and top-level views of passive BCI research leaders to ground the discussion in concrete results. PhD students have the opportunity to present their project in blitz pitches to receive feedback. External collaborators welcome! Please send an email if you would like to contribute.
3. Facilitate a structured forward-looking discussion among participants to collectively identify the field's key open problems and priorities.

**Research insights presentations** will cover topics from research groups working on pBCI with a wide view: meaning, top-level view, challenges, and motivation.

**Structured discussions** will use a facilitated format. Participants will be prompted to respond to a small set of targeted questions and evaluate existing challenges to establish priorities for the field. Responses will be clustered live and used to structure a plenary exchange. The goal is a concrete, shared output to guide the field.
